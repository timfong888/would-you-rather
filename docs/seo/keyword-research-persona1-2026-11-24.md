Source: OpenSEO (DataForSEO, US, Google Ads volumes), pulled 2026-11-24. Semrush was unavailable (account has 0 API units). Volume = US monthly searches, KD = 0-100.

### Does any competitor have a page per individual question? Yes, and it does not rank.
- would-you-rather-game.com has one URL per question, /q/<id>/<slug> (e.g. /q/47987/would-you-rather-be-in-a-relationship-or-to-be-single). Each page: H1 question, A/B with vote percentages, a FAQ block ("What do other players prefer? Out of 12,771 votes, 73%..."), a "part of the selection" link. It claims 2,000+ questions.
- Evidence it doesn't rank: OpenSEO ranked-keyword data for that domain returns only 20 keywords, all on the home page or /lister/sport, at positions 22-83 (best: sports would you rather, pos 33, 320 vol; would you rather game online, pos 47). No /q/ page ranks for any tracked keyword. Treat one-URL-per-question as low-value for head terms; the value is long-tail and internal-link depth.
- would-you-rather.com (600+ questions, categories), rrrather.com, rathervote.com (daily poll, numbered questions) are play-first with no list SEO. wouldyouratherquiz.com (WordPress) publishes themed lists of 67-101 questions with vote totals, including per-age posts for 6, 7 and 8 year olds (published 2026).
- Our angle: question pages should not stand alone as the target. Make each one a spoke of a category or pillar page that carries the rankings.

## 4. Question themes parents search for most (choose the first ~100 question pages)
Ranked by measured demand plus fit, with a suggested allocation:
1. Silly / funny / absurd (18,100 each): about 25 questions (Giggle Factory)
2. Food and snacks (1,000 + 880, also in funny): about 10 (Giggle Factory / Tiny Choices)
3. Gross-out (1,300 + 590): about 8 (Giggle Factory)
4. Holidays: Christmas (2,400 + 1,000), Thanksgiving (1,900), Halloween (2,900), winter 390: about 15 (Holiday Table; Thanksgiving and Christmas first, they peak now)
5. Road trip and car (12,100 for the game family): about 10 (Road Trip Rally), written to be answered aloud with no screen
6. Dinner table and family (880 + 480 + 390): about 10 (Dinner Table)
7. Superpowers, animals, superheroes, dinosaurs (720 + 260 + 140 + 40): about 10 (Make-Believe)
8. Younger kids (kindergarten 720, preschoolers 260, first graders 320): about 7 (Tiny Choices)
9. School and classroom (hidden / 30): about 5 (Giggle Factory / Tiny Choices)
10. Feelings and "get to know you" (emotional intelligence 210, questions to ask your son/daughter 320): about 5 (Heart to Heart)
11. Hard/deep for older kids (hard would you rather 9,900, teens 5,400): about 5 (Real Talk)
Hold back: couples, adult, spicy.
Principle: lead with themes that carry a measured head term (funny, silly, holiday, road trip, gross, food); kids-labelled demand is real but unmeasured, so confirm with Search Console two weeks after launch.
